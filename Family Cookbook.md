## Chocolate Banana Bread

### Ingredients

- 1¼ C all-purpose flour
- ¼ C natural unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1 t baking soda
- 1 t salt
- 1 stick (½ C) butter, RT
- 1 C sugar
- 2 large eggs
- 1 C mashed bananas (2–3 bananas)
- ½ C sour cream
- 1 t vanilla
- ½ C choc chips

### Steps

1. Whisk well the flour, cocoa, baking soda, and salt.
2. Beat the butter and sugar 2–3 min.
3. Add eggs 1 @ a time.
4. Add dry ingred & combine gently.
5. Add bananas, SC & vanilla.
6. Fold in chips.
7. Oven 350. 9x5x3 in pan. Bake \~1h 10min.

## Banana Bread

### Ingredients

- 4 ripe bananas
- 1 C sugar
- 1 egg
- 4 T yogurt / sour cream
- ¼ C melted butter
- 1 t vanilla extract
- 1½ C flour
- ¼ t salt
- 2 t baking powder
- ½ t baking soda
- 1 C chocolate chips

### Steps

1. Mix the flour, salt, baking powder, and baking soda.
2. Beat egg + sugar on medium high for 10 min.
3. Add banana & vanilla. Beat till mixed.
4. Add yogurt & then butter. Beat after each addition.
5. Add flour mix & chocolate chips. Fold in.
6. Place in 8x8. Bake at 350°F \~35 min.

## Ultimate Chocolate Chip Cookies (notes)

### Eggs

- Extra egg whites = taller cookies
- Extra egg yolks = fudgier cookies
- So: 2 whites + 1 yolk → open structure (taller cookie)
- (Xtra H₂O → more gluten formation → taller cookie)
- (3 yolks + 0 whites = denser, fudgier cookie)
- (yolks → emulsified fat → protein coagulum → tender & fudgelike)

### Sugar

- Too much sugar → uniform texture + less cracking. (So maybe cream ½ sugar w/ eggs until completely dissolved, then add rest w/ cooled brown butter.)
- Sugar is hygroscopic. White sugar (sucrose) is mildly hygroscopic + has neutral pH.
- Brown sugar (glucose + fructose) is ↑↑ hygroscopic than sucrose + slightly acidic pH, so w/ baking soda → rise higher.
- ⇒ White sugar = thin, crisp. Brown = tall, moist.
- Therefore: mix the 2 (1:1).
- Substituting some of the white sugar w/ corn syrup (more hygroscopic) leads to soft, \[crossed-out word\], wide, dark & flexible cookies. (10% substitution) more than enough.

### Leavening

- Baking soda (alkaline) = craggy + dense + coarse
- Powder = cakey + smooth (soda + acids)
- Margin: "Brown sugar"

### Flour

- Flour provides bulk of structure to cookie.
- Cake flour = low amount of protein ⇒ less gluten formation → cookies will be ↑↑ tender + mushy.
- All bread flour → ultra-chewy.
- Flour:butter ratio can alter texture:
  - 1:1 = wafer, lace-like cookie
  - 1.3:1 = barely spread, centres stay dense + dough-like
  - 10oz : 8oz

### Other

- Stirring warm dough (80F) alters the chocolate dispersal, i.e. melts the finest pcs but leaves the larger chunks to melt into pockets.
- Temp: the lower the temp, the wider the cookie. E.g. \~275F = cookie is \~homogen\[eous\].
- Let the dough rest for ≤2 days to improve flavour & they bake up darker. This is because flour proteins + starches break down a bit & rearrange.
- Misting cookies = less crisp on top + more cakey \[unclear: softer?\].

## Braised Short Ribs

### Ingredients

- 6 bone-in short ribs (\~5¾ lb)
- S & P & thyme
- Olive oil \[from method\]
- 1 large Spanish onion, finely chopped
- 4 carrots, finely chopped
- 3 celery ribs, finely chopped
- \~2 T tomato paste
- One 750 ml dry red, Cab Sav
- 4 large thyme sprigs
- 1 \[unclear: ½?\] C beef stock
- 3 C broth \[from method\]

### Steps

1. Season ribs. Brown over med-high heat in olive oil \~3–4 min/side until brown + crusted.
2. Cook the onion, carrots, and celery over low heat, \~20 min.
3. Add \~2 T tomato paste. Cook until deep red.
4. Add the wine, thyme sprigs, and beef stock.
5. Pour hot marinade over ribs & let cool. Cover & refrigerate 12–24h.
6. Add 3 C broth to the marinade & boil until reduced by half.
7. Return ribs to sauce. Cover w/ parchment paper & lid. Place in oven (375) \~3h. Turn @ least once. Remove lid last 20–30 min of cooking to brown top.
8. Serve w/ risotto, mashed potatoes or crusty bread.

### Ingredients

## Kitchen Notes

### Bread flour from AP flour

(Av. bread flour protein − AP flour protein) × 1% (recipe flour amt in g) = amt of vital wheat gluten to add.

E.g. 448g bread flour; protein count (on bag) is 12%; AP flour ave. = 9%. (12 − 9) x 4.48 = 13.44g gluten.

### Conversions

- 1 C AP flour = 120g
- 7g instant yeast = ¼ oz instant yeast = 2¼ t instant yeast = 21g fresh yeast or 1¼ blocks (0.6 oz size) or \~½ block (2 oz) fresh
- 1 t instant yeast (3g) = ½ block (0.6 oz size) fresh
- 7g active dry yeast = 2¼ t active = 17.5g fresh = 1 block (0.6 oz size) or ⅓ block (2 oz) fresh
- 1 t active dry yeast = ⅓ block (0.6 oz) fresh
